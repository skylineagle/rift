How much can Rift carry?

We pushed framed, sequence-numbered data through every pair of Rift sides, TCP, UDP and UART, at rising speeds, and counted what came out the other end. This is the short version.

Rift never slows a sender downWhen data arrives faster than the far side can take it, Rift drops 4 KB chunks. Your sender sees no error.
Out of a serial port is easyUART → network carried about 60 MB/s with under 1% loss. A real 115200 baud line is 11.5 KB/s.
Into a serial port is the trapNetwork → UART loses data as soon as the network side outpaces the port. On a real line that means anything over its baud rate.

The question that started this

A UART on side B, a TCP client on side A. How fast can it go?

Side A is a TCP server, your app connects to it. Side B is a serial port. Measured on the Linux box, the only place a pseudo-terminal can stand in for a UART.

UART (B) → TCP server (A) → your client ≈ 60 MB/s

Clean through 50 MB/s, and never worse than about 1% loss even flat out. A real 115200 baud port tops out at 11.5 KB/s, about 5,000 times less. Your client will get every byte the serial device sends.

your client → TCP server (A) → UART (B) ≤ 10 MB/s

The reverse is where it breaks. Clean to 10 MB/s into a pty, 16% lost at 50 MB/s, 90% lost flat out. Your client still writes at full speed and gets no error. On a real port, keep the sender under the baud rate yourself.

Why it drops instead of slowing down

A 1 MB bucket between the two sides

Each direction has a queue of 256 slots, 4 KB each. Rift reads a chunk, tries to put it in the queue, and if the queue is full it throws the chunk away and keeps reading. It never stops reading, so TCP flow control never reaches your sender.

writes One megabyte is about 5 ms of traffic at 200 MB/s. Any short hiccup on the receiving side fills it, which is why loss starts in bursts below the ceiling, not as a clean cutoff.

Two things follow. A fast TCP sender gets a stream with holes cut at random offsets, so anything framed on top sees corruption. And the sender cannot tell: as far as it knows, every write succeeded.

At light load every path adds about 50 µs over a direct connection (p50 around 110 to 130 µs on macOS). A 110-minute soak at about 120 MB/s total held memory flat between 17 and 36 MB. Rift is stable. The limit is rate, not time.

Fine print

Everything ran over loopback on one machine, with the test harness competing with Rift for CPU. Frames were 1 KB, 4 seconds per step, a fresh rift pipe per run, logging set to --quiet. Loss means frames sent minus frames received, cut or spliced frames count as lost.

The Linux machine is a 2 vCPU cloud VM, so it shows what an edge box can do, not a fair match for the 11-core laptop. The UART stand-in is a pseudo-terminal, which has no baud limit, so UART numbers are Rift's own ceiling. A real line is slower than every one of them.

UDP numbers on macOS stop near 200 MB/s because that is where the harness itself tops out with 1 KB datagrams. The full report, with latency, frame sizes, CPU and the soak, is bench/REPORT.html.
